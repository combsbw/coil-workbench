# Cluster 6 — Vascular, wound-healing, muscle and recovery: PEMF/ELF therapeutic-threshold dossier

Source: articles retrieved from PubMed (NCBI) via the PubMed MCP tools in this session (about 35 distinct queries, plus PMC full text where the tool returned any). DOIs are shown as returned by the tool. Where a DOI was not returned it is marked "DOI not returned".

Conventions
- NR = not reported in the abstract or in any full text I could retrieve. Most PMC full-text calls returned only the abstract, so "NR" often means "not in the abstract". Numbers are never filled in from memory.
- "(calc.)" = computed by me. Formulas: sinusoid dB/dt_peak = 2πf·B_peak; pulse dB/dt ≈ B_pk / t_rise; uniform B over a circular loop of radius r gives E = (r/2)·dB/dt, i.e. E[V/m] ≈ 0.005 × r[cm] × dB/dt[T/s]. Sinusoid-equivalent values are an assumption wherever the true waveform is NR, and they are only a rough scale.
- "(via review)" = the number comes from a secondary source (Mayrovitz 2022 full text, PMC9184174), not from the primary paper.
- Quality: H/M/L is my overall rating of the evidence for threshold purposes (design, blinding, parameter reporting, replication), not of the paper's general merit.

## 2. Synthesis per target

### 2.1 Endothelial FGF-2 / VEGF / VEGFR2 / eNOS / PI3K-Akt and angiogenesis
- Range with positive reports: B 1.2 to 5 mT peak, f 15 to 75 Hz, pulses of roughly 1.3 to 40 ms (when reported), 45 min to 8 h/day for 1 to 4 weeks in vivo; 4 to 24 h continuous in cells. Lowest B giving a positive result: 1.5 mT (Peng 2020 at 15 Hz; Vincenzi 2020 at 75 Hz). Lowest sinusoid-equivalent dB/dt with a positive result: about 0.14 T/s (Peng 15 Hz/1.5 mT, calc.); the IGEA signal gives about 2 T/s (calc.).
- Amplitude window and opposite sign: Delle Monache (2 mT, 50 Hz sinusoid, 0.63 T/s calc.) reduced endothelial proliferation, tubulogenesis and VEGFR2, i.e. inside the B and dB/dt range where the pulsed protocols above are pro-angiogenic. The difference is waveform (continuous sinusoid vs pulse bursts), duty and duration, not B. Průcha reports waveform dependence directly (rectangular 340 µs pulses at 72 Hz, 4 mT, changed proliferation; the 5 kHz AM sinusoid mode did not, per abstract). Sheikh reports frequency sensitivity in a direct E-field system.
- Driver: not established. Peng 2020 (15 Hz/1.5 mT vs 30 Hz/3.0 mT) is the only retrieved paired comparison, and B, f and dB/dt rose together (calc. 4x dB/dt), so no variable can be isolated. The FGF-2 signal (Tepper, Callaghan, Goto) and the PI3K-Akt-eNOS signal (Li, Hao, Peng) are blocked by pathway inhibitors, so the mechanism is real in those systems; the activating physical quantity is unresolved. Evidence points to time-varying induced E/J plus waveform/timing rather than static B, but this is inference, not demonstration.
- Nulls and caveats: Goto (VEGF unchanged in marrow, FGF-2 and Ang-2 up); PEMF alone did not rescue HUVEC apoptosis (Cheng 2017); sustained 50 Hz 2 mT sinusoid is inhibitory (Delle Monache); pro-angiogenic gene induction in RPE cells (Oladnabi) is a safety flag.
- Confidence: low-moderate (consistent direction in one preclinical group's repeated 30 Hz/5 mT protocol and in the FGF-2 papers from one other group; no replicated dose-response; clinical parameters rarely reported).

### 2.2 HIF-1α / hypoxia axis
- Peng 2020 and Hao 2014 report HIF-1α up with VEGF. Vincenzi 2020 (1.5 mT, 75 Hz, 1.3 ms, 24 h) found VEGF release 3.2x that is HIF-1α-independent (chetomin-insensitive, HIF-1α protein unchanged). So HIF-1α involvement is tissue- and protocol-dependent.
- Threshold: no amplitude series. Confidence: low.

### 2.3 Microvascular blood flow and vasodilation (low vs high dose)
- Positive: Bragin (rat cortex; RF-modulated, E about 6 V/m in tissue, NO-dependent; effect small, +5.5% RBC velocity, arterioles +10%, 3 h duration); Sun 2016 (0.5 mT, 12 Hz; vein velocity yes, laser-Doppler flux no); Kwan 2015 (1.2 mT, 12 Hz, n=13); Alghadier 2026 (2-5 mT, 15-50 Hz, no sham arm); Stewart 2020 (FMD in hypertension).
- Nulls: Schuhfried 2005 (100 µT/30 Hz and 8.4 mT/10 Hz both null: no dose-response in the healthy foot over 84-fold B and 28-fold dB/dt); McNamee 2010 (200 µT, 60 Hz); Biermann 2020 (BEMER; both legs changed equally, a time/rest artefact); Mayrovitz 2025 (7.8 Hz magnet motor lowered perfusion).
- Static B: threshold about 500 mT for capillary-velocity reduction (Brix), unrelated to dB/dt.
- Reading: effects, where present, are small and appear more reliably in impaired tissue (diabetes, ischaemia) than in healthy skin; healthy-skin laser-Doppler studies do not show a low-dose/high-dose gradient. The only mechanistically controlled study (Bragin) is driven by E-field/NO with B not even defined. This weakens "B amplitude" as the driver and fits "induced E (or SAR-free field intensity) plus timing".
- Confidence: low.

### 2.4 Fibroblast / keratinocyte proliferation, collagen and wound closure
- Cell data: 1 mT/50 Hz (HaCaT growth up, NF-κB down), 1.5 mT/75 Hz/8 h/day (tendon cell collagen I and VI up, proliferation markers down). The only quantitative window is current density: Cleary 1988 found maximal fibroplasia at 3.5 to 7 mA/m² time-averaged (1 ms pulses, 1 Hz), none at 1.8 mA/m² and inhibition above 10 mA/m², an inverted U about 4-fold wide. Direct E-field data (Korraah 1 to 10 V/m raises proliferation and apoptosis; Wolf-Goldberg 1000 V/m) bracket a far higher E than the induced E of typical PEMF (about 0.01 V/m at r = 1 cm for the IGEA signal, calc.), so these direct-field results cannot be used as PEMF thresholds.
- Animal wound models: 1.2 to 8 mT, 15 to 25 Hz, 1 to 8 h/day gave faster early closure (Callaghan 11 vs 15 d; Cheing early benefit that disappeared by end-point). The diabetic-rat 5 mT/25 Hz result is transient.
- Clinical: Cochrane (venous ulcer, 3 RCTs; pressure ulcer, 2 RCTs) finds no robust benefit; the DFU RCTs are tiny (n=13 and n=34) or unsham-controlled (n=66).
- Driver: no study here varies B, f or pulse shape independently for wound endpoints. Duration matters (continuous 24 h 50 Hz 7 mT raised cytokines while 1 h/day did not, via review).
- Confidence: preclinical low-moderate; clinical low.

### 2.5 Skeletal muscle (myoblast differentiation, satellite cells, recovery)
- In vitro: 1.5 mT single 10 min (Yap 2019, TRPC1-Ca2+-NFAT, PGC-1α); 2 mT, 75 Hz, 1.3 ms, 2 h/day best in 3D, 22 h/day not better and perhaps worse, and late (post-fusion) start best (Terrie 2022). Static µT fields (60-400 µT) also accelerate myotube formation via ryanodine-receptor Ca2+ release (Surma 2014), so a B-only (static) route exists in this cell type; a static-field null exists in human satellite cell/MSC co-cultures (Mueller 2017).
- Driver: Terrie shows that timing/duty (daily hours, onset relative to fusion) changes the sign of the 3D result at a fixed B, f and pulse. B vs dB/dt: not tested; dB/dt NR.
- In vivo: DOMS RCT (Jeon 2014) improved soreness, median frequency and electromechanical delay, not torque; parameters NR. No CK-based PEMF study was retrieved (the PubMed query returned zero). rPMS strength gain (Ogawa 2019) is a high-dB/dt neural-stimulation mechanism and should not be pooled with low-dB/dt PEMF.
- Confidence: in vitro low-moderate; in vivo low.

### 2.6 Lymphatic / oedema
- Nothing retrieved on lymphatic function. The one randomised oedema trial retrieved (post-blepharoplasty, pulsed RF) was null on oedema. Pilla's review proposes a Ca/CaM-NO mechanism with no thresholds. Confidence: very low.

### 2.7 Tissue oxygenation
- Bragin: NADH autofluorescence -5.3% (improved oxygenation) lasting about 3 h; Callaghan (via abstract): tissue O2 tension in diabetic flaps comparable to normal. Parameters for amplitude dependence NR. Confidence: low.

### 2.8 Iron / HIF axis
- No paper retrieved links PEMF to iron handling in vascular or muscle tissue. A 15 Hz/3.82 mT, 40 min/day, 2-month protocol changed neither ferritin, hepcidin nor tissue iron (Yang 2025); ELF-induced ferritin chains were reported in MSCs without parameters (Lee 2015). Confidence: very low.

### 2.9 NO / eNOS node (common to vascular targets)
- Lowest B with an eNOS/NO-dependent result: 0.6 mT, 50 Hz (He 2022, osteoblasts); in vivo vasodilation NO-dependent at an RF E-field of about 6 V/m (Bragin). Serum NO did not change in hypertensives (Stewart), so systemic NO is a poor readout. Confidence: moderate that NO mediates, low that any particular field quantity activates it.

### 2.10 Overall answer: B versus dB/dt versus timing (this cluster)
- Not demonstrated. No retrieved study varies B, dB/dt (or induced E/J) and frequency independently while holding the others fixed for a vascular, wound or muscle endpoint. Most devices change B and f together, and dB/dt, induced E and pulse rise time are almost never reported (the 2026 systematic review says the same).
- Indirect evidence that peak B alone is not the driver: (i) NO-dependent microvascular responses occur under an RF field defined by tissue E (6 V/m) with SAR 40 mW/kg and no stated B (Bragin); (ii) healthy-skin flow shows no response across 100 µT to 8.4 mT (Schuhfried) or at 200 µT/60 Hz; (iii) the same 2 mT/50 Hz sinusoid is anti-angiogenic while pulsed 1.5-5 mT bursts are pro-angiogenic (waveform/duty dependence); (iv) the only quantitative window is in current density, an inverted U (Cleary: effective 3.5-7, inactive 1.8, inhibitory >10 mA/m² time-averaged); (v) Terrie shows daily exposure time and onset change the outcome at fixed field; (vi) the 2026 review separates regimes by dB/dt (>60 kT/s) rather than B.
- Indirect evidence that B can matter on its own: static fields act at 60-400 µT (Ca2+ release in muscle cells, Surma) and at about 500 mT (capillary velocity, Brix), where dB/dt is zero.
- Working conclusion for dosing: for pulsed or low-frequency fields the data are best explained by time-varying induced E/J with a waveform/duty/duration window, with positive protocols spanning peak B 0.5 to 5 mT and sinusoid-equivalent dB/dt of about 0.04 to 0.94 T/s (about 2 T/s for the IGEA pulse, calc.); an inverted-U in amplitude is likely. Confidence in this conclusion: low.

## 4. Gaps and caveats
- Not retrieved: McKay's review on EMF and blood flow, Smith's vasodilation studies, Kwan's other rat/human microcirculation papers, Pan et al. and any Goto vascular paper beyond the 2010 marrow study; the PubMed search is conjunctive and returned zero hits for several natural-language queries (including lymphedema, creatine kinase, "dose" queries).
- PMC full text came back empty for Yap, Li and Sheikh; their exposure parameters beyond the abstract are NR here.
- Hao 2014 and Li 2015 share a group and a protocol (30 Hz, 5 mT, 4 x 8 min); they are not independent replications.
- Nearly all "dB/dt" numbers in the tables are sinusoid-equivalent estimates (calc.); true pulse rise times would give larger peak dB/dt for pulsed waveforms. Induced E depends on geometry; use E ≈ 0.005 × r[cm] × dB/dt[T/s] with the tissue cross-section radius.
