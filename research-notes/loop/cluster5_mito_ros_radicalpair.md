# Cluster 5: Mitochondria, redox, and field-strength-driven (B-dependent) mechanisms

Source: all papers below were retrieved via the PubMed MCP tools in this session (search_articles, get_article_metadata, get_full_text_article). According to PubMed, the metadata/abstract text was the basis for every entry; full text was read for only five papers (PMC10604793 Franco-Obregon 2023 review; PMC6353618 Van Huizen 2019; PMC5172244 Usselman 2016; PMC11641689 Merighi 2024; PMC6168118 Sherrard 2018). Yap 2019 (PMC6902701) returned an empty full-text body, so its waveform details are NOT verified. DOIs are given as https://doi.org/<doi>.

Conventions: "NR" = not reported (in the abstract or text I could access). "computed by me" = my calculation. Formulas used:
- sinusoid: dB/dt_peak = 2 pi f B_peak (assumes the quoted B is the peak value; if it is RMS, multiply by 1.414)
- uniform B over a circular loop of radius r: E = (r/2) dB/dt (r = 1 cm for a culture well, 5 cm for a tissue-scale loop; r = 10 um for a single cell)
- electron Larmor frequency = 28.02 MHz per mT; kT(310 K) = 4.28e-21 J.
Every computed E value is an order-of-magnitude estimate for a uniform field in a homogeneous conductor, not a measurement.

## 2. Synthesis per target

### 2.0 Overall answer: what drives activation in this cluster?

1. Static-field experiments (dB/dt = 0, E = 0) change ROS, mitochondrial Ca2+/MMP, growth and differentiation at 0.1-20 mT, and even when the ambient field is removed (hypomagnetic, below 5 uT). Examples: Van Huizen 2019 (100-500 uT), Gurhan 2021 (100-600 uT), Bekhite 2010/2012 (0.4-2 mT vs 10 mT), Alipour 2022 (20 mT SMF), Fu 2016 and Reed 2026 (near-zero field). So the field amplitude B (and its orientation) is sufficient for ROS/mitochondrial effects; induced E is not required. Confidence: moderate.
2. Time-variation adds something, but it is small and unquantified. At identical peak B, static vs 50/60/120 Hz fields give different outcomes in the two paired comparisons I found: Alipour (pHi and viability diverge) and Wang 2018 (ATP up/neutral with static, down/neutral with 50 Hz, variable with 120 Hz). No paper retrieved varied dB/dt at constant B, or B at constant dB/dt, in a PEMF therapy waveform. So dB/dt-versus-B partitioning is unresolved for PEMF.
3. For the signature PEMF finding, TRPC1-Ca2+-mitochondrial activation (Franco-Obregon group), the driver is unidentified. The papers never state dB/dt or induced E in the abstracts; the review itself says rate of change and symmetry are seldom disclosed. Strong data show a molecular requirement (TRPC1) and a developmental timing requirement, not a physical one.
4. Frequency windows appear only in (a) RF-at-Larmor RPM experiments (MHz, not ELF), (b) one unreplicated sub-uT study (Bajtos 2025, 16.5 Hz), and (c) duration/amplitude "efficacy windows" in the TRPC1 literature. Timing (exposure duration, timing relative to TRPC1 expression, weekly schedule) matters in the TRPC1 literature.
5. Most "positive" results come from a small number of lineages (Franco-Obregon/NUS for TRPC1; Barnes-Martino-Usselman-Castello-Gurhan for weak/RF fields; Cadossi-Varani-Gessi for IGEA neuroprotection). Independent replication was not retrieved for any of the three lineages; the nulls (Nieminen, Fei, Goh, Wang 2018) are few, probably because null studies are published less.

### 2.1 TRPC1 / Ca2+ / mitochondrial membrane potential / ATP ("magnetic mitohormesis")

- Threshold / range: effective 1.5 mT (peak), 10 min single dose in myoblasts (Yap 2019); 2 mT, 10 min in hDPSC (Madanagopal 2021); 1.5 mT, 10 min/week in mice (Tai 2020); 1.5 mT, 50 Hz, 5 x 10 min in quail (Yap 2025). A 3 mT, 60 min paradigm produces a different developmental outcome (review PMID 37892906). Minimum effective B and ineffective range: not reported (no amplitude series in any retrieved abstract). Frequency: "15 Hz" in the review vs "50 Hz" in the quail paper; waveform, rise time and symmetry not reported.
- Driver: unresolved. What is solid: (i) TRPC1 is necessary (CRISPR knockdown; Kurth 2020 vesicles show TRPC1 membrane machinery is sufficient to respond to PEMF with Ca2+ entry), (ii) aminoglycosides block it (Yap 2019, Madanagopal 2021, Iversen 2024), (iii) timing: effect tracks TRPC1 expression and is weaker after the log phase, (iv) field direction (upward vs downward) matters in quail embryos and in adherent cells in static fields (review) which points to a vector (B-orientation) dependence, but a unipolar pulse reversal flips both B and dE/dt sign so this does not separate B from dB/dt.
- Ambient-field result: removing the ambient field slowed myogenesis and lowered TRPC1 (Yap 2019); this is a sub-50 uT B-dependent effect with no dB/dt, consistent with the hypomagnetic literature (Fu 2016, Tian 2024).
- Nulls / replication: none retrieved; no independent lab found. Review authors admit variability from ambient field, temperature, density, aminoglycosides.
- Computed (by me): at 1.5 mT and 15-50 Hz sinusoid, dB/dt_peak = 0.14-0.47 T/s, E about 0.7-2.4 mV/m (r = 1 cm) or 3.5-12 mV/m (r = 5 cm), but pulse edges are likely faster than a sinusoid, so these are lower bounds.
- Confidence: moderate that a TRPC1-dependent Ca2+/mitochondrial response exists in this lab's system; low on driver; low on translation.

### 2.2 ROS generation and scavenging (SOD, catalase, antioxidant response)

- Direction is context dependent.
  - Increases: Wolf 2005 (0.5-1 mT, 50 Hz; antioxidant-reversible), Sherrard 2018 (1.8 mT pulses, 10 Hz; cryptochrome-dependent), Bekhite 2010/2012 (static 0.4-2 mT; NOX4/Ca2+), Alipour 2022 (20 mT static and 50 Hz), Yuan 2020 (5.1 mT), Sun 2024 (1 mT, senescent cells only), Manikonda 2014 (50-100 uT chronic, dose-dependent, harmful), Van Huizen 2019 (500 uT), Gurhan 2021 (non-monotonic).
  - Decreases: Merighi 2024/2025 (1.5 mT IGEA pulses reduce ROS in cells that were pre-stressed with H2O2/Abeta/LPS), Liao 2025 (4 mT, 80 Hz), Iversen 2024, Van Huizen (200 uT), Martino 2011 (hypo-field).
  - The pattern: field-induced ROS elevation in unstressed cells; normalisation or reduction when oxidative stress is already present. This fits hormesis but is not proved by any single study, because none showed both directions within one design.
- Amplitude windows: Bekhite 2010 is the cleanest, showing 0.4-2 mT (VEGF, differentiation up) vs 10 mT (inhibitory) with ROS high at both. Van Huizen: reduction at 100-400 uT, increase at 500 uT. Gurhan: H2O2 up at 100-200, down at 300-400, up at 500-600 uT. Goh 2025: proliferation plateaus at 14 mT (none further at 16 mT), with no ROS involvement. I could not verify a "1.5 mT bell-shaped curve" in the retrieved abstracts; the NUS review only states that graded efficacy windows exist.
- Nulls: Nieminen 2024 (100 and 500 uT, 50 Hz: no ROS change), Fei 2019 (0-4 mT medium), Goh 2025 (10-16 mT: ROS unchanged and not required), Wang 2018 (ATP changes not correlated with ROS or MMP).
- Driver: B alone is sufficient for ROS changes (static experiments). Time-varying fields at the same B do not give identical outcomes (Alipour; Wang). In both, dB/dt is a modifier, not a prerequisite. Nothing retrieved supports an induced-E mechanism for ROS at 1-20 mT: the computed macroscopic E in a 1 cm culture well is about 1e-4 to 3e-2 V/m (0.16-31 mV/m across the 50-60 Hz studies above), which is below the Weaver-Astumian broad-band limit (0.1 V/m) and, for the lowest-amplitude studies, near or below the narrow-band limit (1e-4 V/m). Static fields have zero E yet still change ROS.
- Confidence: moderate that fields in the 0.1-20 mT range change ROS under some conditions; low on the sign and reproducibility; low on dB/dt role.

### 2.3 Nrf2 / HO-1 / antioxidant response

- Evidence: Patruno 2020 (50 Hz, 1 mT; HO-1 peaks at 6 h; Nrf2 nuclear entry needs Akt and ERK), Alipour 2022 (20 mT static and 50 Hz both raise Nrf2, SOD2, GST), Zhai 2023 (PEMF, nuclear Nrf2 and GSH-Px up in NAFLD mice; SOD and CAT unchanged), Turan 2026 and Bindal 2026 (rat LPS models; parameters NR), Lin 2025 (engineered nanoparticles plus 1 kHz field).
- Threshold: shortest tested exposure was 1 h at 1 mT (50 Hz); no amplitude series; no ineffective range reported. Static 20 mT also works, so Nrf2 induction is not specific to time-varying fields.
- Driver: Nrf2 follows ROS (secondary), so it inherits the B-driven ROS logic above. Direct field-Nrf2 coupling is not shown.
- Nulls: none retrieved (likely publication bias; the Nieminen ROS null at 100 and 500 uT implies no Nrf2 drive there, but Nrf2 was not measured).
- Confidence: low-moderate (consistent direction, few independent papers, parameters often not in abstracts).

### 2.4 Mitochondrial respiration, MMP, ATP

- Evidence is directionally inconsistent: PEMF 1.5 mT raises respiration and mitochondriogenesis (Yap 2019, Tai 2020); 4 mT, 80 Hz shifts fibroblasts toward glycolysis (Liao 2025); IGEA pulses preserve MMP under insult (Merighi); 6 mT static/50 Hz/120 Hz give small, inconsistent ATP changes (Wang 2018); hypo-field lowers MMP and ATP (Fu 2016); static 2 mT lowers MMP with exposure time (Calabro 2020); static 140 mT for 72 h reportedly attenuates OXPHOS (cited in the NUS review); 20 mT is best for oocyte MMP in a 20-80 mT series (Pargoo 2024); Usselman 2016: orientation of an RF field at Zeeman resonance gives OCR +54% (parallel) vs ECAR +100% (perpendicular).
- Driver: both static B and orientation can shift bioenergetics (Fu; Calabro; Usselman). Time variation modulates (Wang).
- Confidence: low (heterogeneous cell types, assays, and no common parameter set).

### 2.5 Radical-pair mechanism (flavin / superoxide, cryptochrome)

- Field magnitude needed: demonstrated sensitivity down to <= 50 uT in a model chemical system (Maeda 2008), and for ROS partitioning between 20 nT and 50 uT (Austvold 2024: H2O2 -13%, O2.- +10%). Magnetic-field effects rise as B approaches the hyperfine field and saturate above it (hyperfine fields of flavin radicals are in the 0.1-few mT range; this is background knowledge, not from a retrieved paper). The Binhi 2025 criterion gamma H tau > 1 + kappa tau, evaluated by me with gamma = 1.76e11 rad/s/T and neglecting the kappa tau term, gives 0.09-0.9 at 50 uT (tau = 10-100 ns) but 1.8-18 at 1 mT and 2.6-26 at 1.5 mT: milli-tesla fields satisfy it more readily than geomagnetic fields.
- Does frequency / dB/dt matter? Spin coherence times are ns-us. A 10-100 Hz field is therefore quasi-static for the radical pair: the yield follows the instantaneous B and the cycle-average matters, not dB/dt. Resonant (frequency-selective) effects need RF at the Larmor frequency (28 MHz per mT; 1.4 MHz at 50 uT) with an amplitude of 1-10 uT (Usselman, Castello, Gurhan 2023; Vacha 2009 with 12-18 nT onset). A 10-100 Hz pulse train does not contain energy at the Larmor frequency of a mT bias unless edges are ns-fast. Resonance at ELF would require a near-zero bias (electron Larmor at 1 nT is about 28 Hz, computed by me), which a bipolar waveform crosses only momentarily. Barnes & Greenebaum propose Hz-MHz windows through nuclear / hyperfine transitions but give no verified ELF window for a mT-level coil. Bajtos 2025 reports a sharp ELF window (16.5 Hz at 10 uT on 45 uT) that does not match the electron Larmor frequency (1.26 MHz) or the proton Larmor frequency (about 1.9 kHz, computed by me); it is unexplained and unreplicated.
- Biological support: Sherrard 2018 (cryptochrome-dependent ROS rise with a 10 Hz, 1.8-2 mT pulse, with controls), Usselman 2014/2016, Castello 2014 (MHz RF + 45-50 uT), Van Huizen 2019 (static, sign of the effect depends on B), Thoeni 2024 (tNMR, superoxide central). Counter-evidence: Wang 2019 argues polarity sensitivity excludes an axial radical-pair compass in human brain; Goh 2025 finds ROS irrelevant for a 14 mT proliferation effect; no independent replication of the HT-1080 results was retrieved.
- Applicability to a coil delivering 0.5-5 mT, pulsed at 10-100 Hz: B in range is large enough to alter radical-pair yields (qualitatively) but there is no frequency selectivity; effect is a function of the waveform's B distribution (peak, time at peak, sign) rather than of dB/dt. Magnitude of the effect expected from the Austvold-type numbers is order 10% change in ROS product ratios, not a switch.
- Confidence: low-moderate that RPM contributes to ROS changes at uT-mT; low that it explains PEMF therapy results.

### 2.6 Magnetite torque and mechanosensitive channels (Piezo1, TRPV, TRPC)

- Magnitude needed (computed by me; Ms = 4.8e5 A/m, textbook value, cube geometry): magnetic energy m B / kT for one single-domain magnetite crystal: 30 nm 3.0 at 1 mT; 50 nm 14 at 1 mT and 0.7 at 50 uT; 80 nm 57 at 1 mT. So at 0.5-5 mT a single 50 nm crystal carries 7-70 kT, enough for torque transduction; Kirschvink 1992 finds >= 5e6 crystals/g in clumps of 50-100 in human brain, so geomagnetic-strength torque (0.7 kT per crystal, much more for chains) is already marginal and mT-range torque is clearly above thermal noise.
- Frequency / dB/dt: torque scales with B, not dB/dt. Time structure matters only through gate and cytoskeleton mechanics; in the Kirschvink model AC below about 10 Hz has minimal effect, while pulsed or DC fields can still force gates open (Dobson 1996, per abstracts).
- Evidence in cells / tissue: none for PEMF therapy. Piezo1 search with "pulsed electromagnetic field" returned zero hits; hits with nanoparticles / nanomagnets involve engineered transducers (Choi 2024; Hao 2019). Meister 2016 shows that ferritin-based magnetogenetic claims fail by 5-10 orders of magnitude; Barbic 2019 argues for alternatives; unresolved. Wang 2019 (human EEG) supports ferromagnetic sensing at Earth-strength fields but is single-lab.
- Applicability to 0.5-5 mT coil: physically possible for biogenic magnetite where it exists, with B (not dB/dt) the driver, but no PEMF-therapy evidence in this search.
- Confidence: low (theory only; positive human data single lab; PEMF-specific data absent).

### 2.7 Diamagnetic anisotropy / orientation

- Magnitude needed: about 1 T for ordered membranes (Hong 1995), 8-14 T for cell alignment (Umeno 2003), 9 T for EGFR kinase orientation (Zhang 2016; MD 1-9 T), 16 T for Tau phase-separation effects (Lin 2023). Orientation energy scales as B squared: a 5 mT coil is 2.5e-5 of a 1 T field's energy and 2.8e-8 of a 9 T field (computed by me).
- Frequency / dB/dt: irrelevant (static-field effect; gradient force can even suppress alignment, Umeno 2003).
- Applicability to 0.5-5 mT: none.
- Confidence: high for the strong-field effect; high that it is inapplicable at mT.

### 2.8 Zeeman splitting and ion cyclotron resonance

- Direct Zeeman energy: mu_B B / kT = 2.2e-6 at 1 mT and 1.1e-5 at 5 mT (computed by me), so Zeeman energy cannot act thermodynamically; only spin-selective chemistry (RPM) can convert it into a biochemical signal.
- Ca2+ cyclotron frequency (computed by me) = qB/(2 pi m) = 0.766 Hz/uT. The NUS review mentions a report (NT2 cells, 2.5 uT at 7 Hz on 10 uT static) matching this ratio; no independent replication retrieved; in a coil with 0.5-5 mT B, cyclotron resonance would sit at about 380-3800 Hz, not at the 10-100 Hz repetition rate.
- Confidence: low.

### 2.9 Weaver-Astumian / Adair limits applied to a 0.5-5 mT, 10-100 Hz coil

Weaver & Astumian (1990, PMID 2300806): cells can respond directly to about 1e-3 V/cm = 0.1 V/m in a broad band, or about 1e-6 V/cm = 1e-4 V/m if the response is narrow-band or enzymatically signal-averaged. Computed by me (E = (r/2) dB/dt, sinusoid):

| Coil case | dB/dt_peak | E, r = 10 um (single cell) | E, r = 1 cm (culture well) | E, r = 5 cm (tissue loop) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5 mT, 10 Hz | 0.031 T/s | 1.6e-7 V/m | 1.6e-4 V/m | 7.9e-4 V/m |
| 1.5 mT, 15 Hz | 0.14 T/s | 7e-7 V/m | 7e-4 V/m | 3.5e-3 V/m |
| 1.5 mT, 50 Hz | 0.47 T/s | 2.4e-6 V/m | 2.4e-3 V/m | 1.2e-2 V/m |
| 5 mT, 100 Hz | 3.1 T/s | 1.6e-5 V/m | 1.6e-2 V/m | 7.9e-2 V/m |
| 1.5 mT IGEA pulse (Merighi, about 2 T/s computed from probe) | about 2 T/s | about 1e-5 V/m | about 1e-2 V/m | about 5e-2 V/m |

Reading: the "r = 10 um" column is only the E generated by flux through a cell-sized loop and is NOT what a cell experiences; a cell in tissue or in a dish sees the macroscopic E set by the whole loop, so compare the 1 cm and 5 cm columns against the limits. Those macroscopic values (0.16 mV/m to about 80 mV/m) are 1.3 to 3 orders below the broad-band limit of 0.1 V/m, and 1.6 to 790 times above the narrow-band limit of 1e-4 V/m. A broad-band direct detector is therefore excluded across the coil's range, but a narrow-band or signal-averaging detector is not excluded at the 1.5 mT / 50 Hz and 5 mT / 100 Hz end; nothing retrieved demonstrates such a detector. By contrast, the B-dependent mechanisms (RPM, magnetite torque) do not rely on induced E and are not excluded by these limits; they are limited by their own physics (section 2.5-2.6). Adair 1991 (PMID 9905119) is retrieved but its abstract is not available; I do not report its numbers.

Limitations: (1) abstracts only for most papers, so waveforms and exposure geometry are often missing; (2) searches returned nothing for Binhi's earlier papers, Kirschvink 1992 Phys Rev A, Player & Hore, Sheppard, Brocklehurst, Engstrom, or Piezo1 + PEMF; (3) Adair 1991/1992 abstracts are unavailable; (4) I did not independently check the values quoted second-hand from the NUS review (15 Hz, 7 mT osteogenesis, 140 mT SMF, Ca-ICR paradigm); (5) all dB/dt and E values marked "computed by me" assume a sinusoid with quoted B as peak, or the stated pick-up-coil geometry.
